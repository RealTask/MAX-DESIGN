# MAX-DESIGN principles

This document describes the reasoning standards shared by the skills.

## 1. Intent over trend

Choose a visual style that supports the product's audience and task. A quiet finance tool, expressive music service, clinical workflow, and playful creator app should not look interchangeable. Trends are ingredients, not a design strategy.

## 2. Hierarchy before decoration

Users should understand what matters, what they can do, and what happens next. Solve structure, copy, grouping, spacing, and type before adding gradients, shadows, glass, particles, or animation.

## 3. Coherence over isolated perfection

A beautiful component can still be wrong if it breaks the system. Use consistent semantic tokens, spacing logic, states, and interaction behavior. Local exceptions need a reason.

## 4. Realistic content

Use representative content: long names, empty results, errors, varied amounts, missing avatars, and translations where relevant. Placeholder text can conceal layout defects and poor information architecture.

## 5. Responsive behavior as a first-class feature

Do not assume desktop can simply be compressed. Reconsider prioritization, information density, navigation, and input behavior at narrow widths. Keep important content accessible through zoom and text resizing.

## 6. Accessibility is design quality

Semantic structure, keyboard support, clear feedback, readable contrast, and reduced-motion alternatives benefit more than a small set of users. Add accessibility while shaping the component, not as a cosmetic pass after completion.

## 7. Functionality over simulation

A control should work, clearly communicate its status, or be explicitly disabled. Do not use fake loading percentages, fake successful submissions, fabricated customer counts, invented testimonials, or other ungrounded claims to make a prototype feel real.

## 8. Performance is part of the interface

Use appropriate image sizes, avoid layout shifts, minimize expensive animation, and add dependencies only when they earn their cost. A polished interaction that makes a page slow or inaccessible is not a quality improvement.

## 9. Preserve evidence and reversibility

Inspect the current system, record uncertainty, and make reviewable changes. Avoid sweeping rewrites based on assumptions. A design decision should be explainable and testable.

## 10. Quality is verified, not declared

Separate code inspection, automated tests, visual browser review, assistive-technology testing, and user research. Each answers different questions. Say what was checked, under what conditions, and what remains unknown.
